# Pre-panel notes — what the author already believes is wrong

*Written 2026-09-14, BEFORE any Rule 7.4 panel has read this page. This
exists so N — the novel share of panel defects — can be measured rather than
reconstructed. Reconstructing it after the fact produces a flattering number
and disarms the retirement trigger.*

**Authorship, stated plainly.** The four charts were built by a Claude Code
session against the Stage 2 brief; Aaron Robbins is the module owner and has
not yet read the rendering. Everything below is what the build session itself
suspects after looking at every render on the K6 ladder (320, 625, 626, 965,
966, 1040). Anything a panel also finds is **not** novel. Defects the session
found and fixed before this file was written are in `chart-review.md` §2, not
here.

## Suspected, chart by chart

**Chart 1 — the two deferral distributions**
1. The shared y-scale (Rule 6.2) is set by the monthly spike of 580, so the
   annual panel's bars reach under a fifth of the panel height. A reader may
   take "spread" for "small", or compare bar heights across the panels as if
   they carried different scales.
2. The monthly panel is one tall bar and eleven empty bins. The empty bins
   may read as missing data rather than as counts of zero.
3. The median reference line is drawn only to the height of the panel's
   tallest bar. Among the annual bars it may read as a tick or a bar outline
   rather than as a reference line.
4. Bins are labelled by their starting day (0, 30, 60 …). A reader may read
   "30" as exactly thirty days rather than as 30–59.
5. The annotation's second clause — "the right tail is partly the window's
   edge" — is a mechanism claim a reader cannot check from the canvas. It is
   defended in the governance note under the chart, which a screenshot does
   not carry (Rule 4.3 tension, accepted knowingly).

**Chart 2 — backlog by term type and cause**
6. At the design width the x-axis runs to $120K so the annotation fits beside
   the first bar. The bars occupy under a third of the plot. A reader may read
   the empty span as scale the data has, or as a defect.
7. "Cancellations riding out the term" is the module's own phrase. At the
   narrow width it abbreviates to "cancellations", which may read as
   "cancelled dollars" rather than as dollars still billing after a cancel.
8. Colour encodes term type; cause is carried by the category label only.
   A reader may look for a colour meaning for cause and not find one.
9. Cancellations are the larger dollars and deferred reductions the larger
   count. The counts are in the value labels, but a reader who reads bar
   length alone will leave saying "it is mostly cancellations".

**Chart 3 — register against effective, month by month**
10. The shaded gap is at most 8.9% of the effective line on an axis that
    includes zero (a ratio claim in the title forces it, Rule 2.1). In the
    first twelve months the band is a few pixels. "Every one of 24 months" is
    true and may not be visible to the eye at the left of the plot.
11. Both lines rise because the book grows through the window. A reader may
    leave with "the gap is growing" — a deterioration reading — when the share
    is roughly flat after the first year. The share is in the subtitle, the
    summary and the table, not on the plot.
12. The register line is Rain beneath the effective line and nearly coincident
    early on. Its end label is the only identification.
13. The annotation names June's gap; the January 2026 peak (6,964 licences,
    8.9%) is unannotated. A reader may ask why the gap fell afterwards, and
    the chart does not say.
14. The green effective line reuses the hue that means "annual term" on
    Charts 1, 2 and 4. A reader who learnt that mapping may read the green
    line as the annual book.

**Chart 4 — derived share by term type**
15. "Billing rows", "renewal no transaction marked" and "manufactured" are the
    module's vocabulary. "Manufactured" may read as pejorative rather than as
    "derived under a stated rule".
16. Two bars is close to a pair of stat tiles. A reader may ask why it is a
    chart at all.

**Across all four**
17. Term-type colours are consistent (annual Evergreen, monthly Glacier) but
    keyed only by direct labels; there is no page-level key, by design (3.6).
18. Nothing is shown per partner or per customer, by decision (no real-book
    reading). The revenue-operations reader the page names will ask "which
    partner", and the page says only that the question is out of scope.
19. At the narrow width every annotation becomes a serif note under the
    provenance strip. It may read as a caption for the strip rather than for
    the chart.
20. The KPI band says "Entitlement gap: 5,875 licences" and Chart 3 says
    "5,875 fewer". The same number in two framings may read as two facts.
21. The decision the page names — stop invoicing from the register — is
    supported by the charts indirectly (they show the gap, not an invoice
    error). A finance reader may ask to see an invoice line that was wrong.

## What I expect the panel to find that I have not listed

Unknown, which is the point of running it. The specific worry is vocabulary:
"deferred", "effective", "register", "manufactured" and "subscription-month"
are each defined once in prose and used many times on canvases that travel
alone.
